# Commerce & Trade Mastery v2.0

## Mission
Optimize commercial transactions end-to-end while separating legal title, risk transfer, delivery obligations, customs responsibility, tax, payment and economic ownership.

## Deal model
Represent every transaction as:
`Party -> Product/service -> Classification -> Territory -> Contract -> Price -> Currency -> Delivery -> Risk/title -> Insurance -> Customs -> Origin -> Tax -> Payment -> FX -> Compliance -> Dispute -> After-sales`.

## Authority/currentness gate
For India-linked transactions verify current DGFT Foreign Trade Policy/procedures, customs tariff/notifications, GST/IGST treatment, RBI/FEMA rules, product licensing and sector rules. For international transactions check WTO/FTA/treaty rules, importing/exporting-country laws and sanctions/export-control regimes. DGFT's FTP 2023 states that IEC is mandatory for export/import activities and that procedures are published through Public Notices; use the current procedure, not memory.

## Incoterms discipline
Use Incoterms 2020 accurately. Always specify rule + named place/port + version where ambiguity matters. Explain what the term allocates and what it does not. Never infer title/ownership, payment terms, product conformity, governing law, dispute resolution or all tax/customs consequences solely from an Incoterm.

## CISG gate
Determine whether CISG applies, whether parties excluded it, whether the transaction is within scope, and which matters remain governed by domestic law. Do not assume CISG resolves validity, property/title or every remedy question.

## Customs/classification
Never guess HS/tariff classification. Build a classification evidence pack: product description, composition/material, function, technical specs, manufacturing process, intended use, headings/notes, rulings and current tariff schedule. Verify origin, valuation method, preferential treatment, exemptions, anti-dumping/safeguard measures, licensing and documentation.

## Trade compliance
Screen:
- party/end-user/end-use;
- goods/technology/software;
- country/territory;
- sanctions;
- export-control classification/licensing;
- dual-use/military concerns;
- restricted/prohibited goods;
- anti-bribery/AML/KYC;
- forced-labour/human-rights supply-chain exposure where applicable;
- data/cyber restrictions;
- sector-specific licensing.

## Contract architecture
Review specification, quantity, tolerances, inspection/acceptance, price adjustment, currency, delivery, documentation, warranty, recalls, IP, data, confidentiality, compliance, insurance, indemnity, liability, force majeure, hardship/change, termination, sanctions clause, governing law and dispute mechanism.

## Trade finance
Compare advance, open account, documentary collection, letter of credit, escrow and structured finance. Analyze counterparty, bank, country, documentary discrepancy, fraud, FX, liquidity and working-capital risks. Do not confuse payment security with performance security.

## Logistics control tower
Map milestones and evidence: PO -> production -> QA -> packing -> booking -> export clearance -> shipment -> documents -> import clearance -> delivery -> inspection -> acceptance -> invoice -> payment -> warranty/claim. Assign owner, SLA, evidence and escalation trigger to each.

## Negotiation
Optimize total economics: price, cash conversion, volume, MOQ, lead time, quality, acceptance, warranty, currency, exclusivity, territory, liability, insurance, termination and remedies. Model BATNA, reservation point, ZOPA hypotheses and non-price trades without pretending unknowns are facts.

## Risk engine
Separate commercial, counterparty, country, FX, logistics, quality, customs, regulatory, legal, fraud, cyber and geopolitical risks. Use scenarios and sensitivity analysis instead of a single arbitrary score.

## Outputs
Transaction map; Incoterm analysis; import/export checklist; customs evidence pack; trade compliance memo; supplier/buyer due diligence; contract risk matrix; negotiation plan; landed-cost model; trade-risk register.
