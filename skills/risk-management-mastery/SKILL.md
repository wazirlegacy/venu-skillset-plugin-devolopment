# Risk Management Mastery v2.0

## Mission
Protect and create value by managing uncertainty against objectives. Risk management must change decisions, capital allocation, controls and resilience—not merely populate a register.

## Framework stack
Use ISO 31000 for general risk principles/process; COSO ERM for strategy/performance integration; IEC 31010 for assessment techniques where applicable; NIST CSF 2.0 for cybersecurity; NIST AI RMF for AI; sector frameworks/regulators when mandatory. Standards are not automatically law.

ISO 31000 emphasizes creating/protecting value, integration, customization, stakeholder/human factors and continual improvement. NIST AI RMF uses Govern, Map, Measure, Manage and is being revised; therefore verify the current version before treating it as definitive.

## Risk statement
`Because [cause], [event] may occur, causing [impact] to [objective] within [time horizon].`
Identify owner, affected objective, velocity, persistence, reversibility and detection ability.

## Taxonomy
Strategic; financial/liquidity/market/credit; operational; people; legal/regulatory; cyber/privacy; AI/model/data; third-party/supply chain; project/change; geopolitical/country; safety/environment; fraud; reputation.

## Assessment rigor
Define likelihood, impact, confidence and evidence. Use ranges when precision is unsupported. Distinguish inherent risk, control effectiveness and residual risk. Avoid multiplying arbitrary 1–5 scores as if they were measured probabilities.

## Appetite and capacity
Separate appetite, tolerance, limit, trigger and capacity. Link each material risk to an objective, resource constraint and decision authority. Regulatory limits must come from the applicable regulator/law, not generic risk matrices.

## Controls
For each material risk define preventive/detective/corrective controls, owner, frequency, evidence, test, failure mode, residual exposure and remediation. Evaluate control design and operating effectiveness separately.

## Scenario engineering
Build severe-but-plausible scenarios, including correlated/common-mode failures and cascading effects. Use reverse stress testing: start from unacceptable outcome and work backward to plausible paths.

## Risk economics
Use expected-loss ranges, stress losses, mitigation cost, insurance/transfer, resilience value, option value and opportunity cost. Run sensitivity and break-even analyses. Never imply a speculative expected value is a fact.

## Resilience
Map critical services, dependencies, recovery objectives, substitutes, manual workarounds, data recovery, third parties, communications and decision authority. Test plans rather than declaring them resilient.

## Cyber/AI
Use NIST CSF 2.0 functions and NIST AI RMF's Govern/Map/Measure/Manage. For AI assess data provenance, model behavior, misuse, privacy, security, human oversight, monitoring, drift, third-party dependencies and incident response. Treat the NIST AI RMF as voluntary guidance unless another rule incorporates it.

## Third-party
Assess concentration, criticality, data/access, subcontractors, geography, financial health, security, SLA, incident obligations, audit rights, continuity, exit/substitutability and lock-in.

## Monitoring
Define KRIs, thresholds, leading indicators, near misses, loss events, control tests, trend breaks and review cadence. Every trigger must map to an action and owner.

## Adversarial review
For every major recommendation ask: what breaks first? what correlations are hidden? what assumption is weakest? what if controls fail together? what is the tail loss? how quickly can we detect it? what decision is reversible?

## Outputs
Enterprise risk register; bow-tie/control map; scenario analysis; reverse stress test; resilience plan; cyber/AI risk assessment; third-party risk; board dashboard; risk appetite proposal; incident post-mortem.
