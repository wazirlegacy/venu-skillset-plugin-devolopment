# Entrepreneurship Mastery v2.0

## Mission
Convert uncertainty into evidence-backed action while preserving optionality, cash, ethics and customer value.

## Competence architecture
Use EntreComp's three areas—Ideas & Opportunities, Resources, Into Action—with its 15 competences and eight progression levels. The expert level is a learning benchmark, not a claim of omniscience.

## Opportunity discovery
Start from customer problems, jobs, constraints, underserved segments, non-consumption, technology/regulatory shifts, distribution advantages and founder means. Separate observations, interpretations and hypotheses.

## Hypothesis system
Write falsifiable assumptions with:
`hypothesis -> evidence -> test -> threshold -> decision -> learning`.
Prioritize assumptions by uncertainty × consequence × testability.

## Customer validation
Use interviews/observation for discovery, behavioral tests for validation, prototypes for usability, preorders/pilots/contracts for commitment, and cohort data for retention. Do not confuse stated intent with revealed preference.

## Business model
Map customer, problem, value proposition, channels, relationships, activities/resources, partners, revenue, costs, working capital, constraints, moat/defensibility and regulatory dependencies.

## MVP/experiments
Build the smallest credible experiment that tests the riskiest assumption. Define success/failure thresholds before running it. Avoid vanity MVPs that only demonstrate technology.

## Unit economics
Model revenue, variable cost, contribution margin, CAC, retention/churn, payback, gross margin, fixed costs, working capital, sales cycle and cash conversion. Run sensitivity and downside cases; reconcile accounting definitions.

## GTM
Select beachhead using pain, urgency, budget, access, competition and economics. Choose sales motion, channel, offer, proof, onboarding and expansion. Validate repeatability before scaling acquisition.

## Finance/capital
Compare bootstrapping, grants, debt, revenue-based financing, angels, venture and strategic capital. Model runway, dilution, covenants, control, liquidation/preferences and downside obligations where applicable. Do not give investment/legal/tax conclusions without current professional verification.

## Legal foundation
Integrate entity, founder agreements, IP assignment, employment/contractor classification, customer/vendor contracts, privacy, tax, insurance, compliance, accounting records and governance into the venture plan.

## Effectuation + causation
Use effectuation under high uncertainty—means, affordable loss, partnerships, contingencies and control. Use causal planning when forecasts become informative. Mature ventures combine both rather than worship one school.

## Scaling
Scale only when a bottleneck is understood. Standardize repeatable processes, automate, hire against constraints, diversify critical dependencies, establish controls and preserve decision speed. Watch quality degradation and organizational complexity.

## Innovation portfolio
Balance core, adjacent and exploratory bets. Use stage gates and evidence. ISO 56002 provides generic innovation-management guidance; a 2026 committee draft is under development to replace the 2019 edition, so distinguish current published guidance from draft material.

## Founder decision OS
For each decision answer: objective; evidence; assumptions; downside/affordable loss; reversibility; fastest reliable test; decision threshold; owner; deadline; post-decision review.

## Adversarial founder review
Ask what a skeptical customer, competitor, investor, regulator, employee and creditor would attack. Identify fatal assumptions and cheapest disconfirming tests.

## Outputs
Opportunity thesis; customer discovery; experiment backlog; business model; MVP plan; unit economics; GTM; funding strategy; hiring plan; operating model; scaling roadmap; innovation portfolio; founder decision memo.
